The Philippines travel guide for UK travellers

Everything you need before you fly home — entry rules, best seasons, money, and what to pack. Written for balikbayans, mixed-nationality families and first-time visitors alike.

🛂 Entry requirements

British citizens can enter the Philippines visa-free for up to 30 days — you’ll just need a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival and proof of onward travel. Extensions are easy to arrange at any Bureau of Immigration office.

Filipino citizens and dual nationals enter freely. Former Filipinos travelling with family may qualify for the Balikbayan Privilege: a free 1-year visa-free stay when arriving together with a Filipino spouse or parent. All travellers must complete the eTravel registration (etravel.gov.ph) within 72 hours before departure — it’s free; ignore any site that charges.

💷 Money matters

  • The currency is the Philippine peso (₱); £1 ≈ ₱70–75 in recent years.
  • Cards are widely accepted in cities and resorts, but carry cash for markets, tricycles, island tours and provincial towns.
  • GCash and Maya dominate local payments — handy if you have a Philippine SIM and ID.
  • ATMs dispense ₱10,000–20,000 per withdrawal with a small fee; airport rates are poor, so change a little and withdraw in the city.

🧳 Packing essentials

  • Light, breathable clothing plus one warm layer for aggressively air-conditioned malls and buses.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen and insect repellent — both cost more locally.
  • Type A/B/C plug adapter; the Philippines runs on 220V.
  • Dry bag for island hopping and sudden habagat showers.
  • Pasalubong space! Fly with 2×23kg and leave one bag half-empty for the trip home.

🗺 Where to go

  • Luzon — Manila’s history and food, Tagaytay’s Taal views, Ilocos heritage, Banaue rice terraces.
  • Visayas — Cebu and Bohol adventures, Boracay’s White Beach, Iloilo’s old churches, Siquijor’s waterfalls.
  • Mindanao — Davao’s Mt Apo, Siargao’s surf, Camiguin’s volcanoes and CDO’s white-water rafting.

🚑 Staying safe & well

  • No mandatory vaccinations for UK arrivals; hepatitis A and typhoid jabs are commonly recommended — check with your GP 6–8 weeks before travel.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water; “buko juice” straight from the coconut is always safe and better.
  • Grab is the reliable way around Metro Manila and Cebu; agree tricycle fares before boarding elsewhere.
  • Follow FCDO travel advice for the latest regional guidance before you book.
